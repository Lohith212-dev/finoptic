## What's in the mock-up now

**The mock-up opens on the sign-in screen** — the product's front door is its home page, and a shared link still overrides it. **20 screens, six datasets, and one assistant layer** — Observability was removed on 30 July; it is still a spend category, just not a screen, still double-click-runnable: no build step, no framework, no server.

**The code is modular.** `app.js` was 2,300 lines and could only be worked on by one person at a time; it is now `core` / `components` / `charts` / `screens` / `shell`, plus `tooltip`, `people`, `catalog`, `tablekit`, `motion` and three `screens-*` files. They are plain `<script>` tags sharing one global scope, so **the load order in `index.html` is the dependency graph** — only `shell.js` runs anything at load, at its foot. One stylesheet; the per-area partials that let five workstreams run in parallel have been folded back into `styles.css` under their own headings.

**Six datasets.** `baseline`, `ai-crisis`, `optimised`, `scaleup`, and two that exist to be difficult: **`fresh`** (Marlowe Bioworks — ten weeks old, two closed months, no anomalies because there is no baseline to deviate from) and **`zero`** (Ashcombe Retail — day one, every total zero and every list *empty*, so cards fall through to real empty states rather than drawing a donut of eight `$0K` slices). Each is a `.js` wrapper around pure JSON, because `fetch()` of a local file is blocked from `file://`. A real `.json` loads via `FileReader`. Export writes CSV of tables *and* row lists with a UTF-8 BOM, plus the dataset as JSON, which round-trips back through the picker. Share copies a URL encoding dataset, screen, view and every filter. The contract and its invariants are in `finoptic/data/SCHEMA.md`.

**Filters are multi-select**, with a custom date range that resolves to whole months and says so. Selecting every value collapses back to "All", because a filter that excludes nothing must not claim to be filtering.

**The period pill is the primary control and is dressed as one** — it carries the brand gradient and the hatch overlay the hero KPI tile used to wear, with no outline, going solid on a custom range while every other pill still fills with ink when set. **There is no hero tile any more**: every KPI tile on a screen is identical, and the gradient moved rather than being added, so the board still spends full-strength accent exactly once per screen. Period is the board's subject rather than a narrowing of it: every figure on every screen is a figure for a span of months, and it is the one dimension with no unset state to be quiet in. Procurement gained the pill in the same pass — its figures always moved with the period ($1.62M → $475K on Last quarter) but the control that moved them was not on the screen. `optimize` and `alerts` still have none, because a backlog and an open-alert feed are not scoped by month and a control that changed nothing would be worse than none.

**Every heading is Title Case, every word capitalised** — 271 strings rewritten at source across pages, cards, KPI labels, column headers, section headings, nav rows, empty states and menus, plus a `titleCase()` helper for the two headings built from data. Acronyms survive by rule rather than by word list: a word carrying an uppercase letter after its first character is left alone, which protects SaaS, AI, YTD, ITSM, EC2, MoM and every brand name. Sentences stay sentences — card sub-lines, hints, notes, the band's prose, field labels, and the assistant's answer headlines. `text-transform: uppercase` remains banned outright; that is a separate rule and it did not change.

**Every ranked plot, list and table reads highest-first.** The sort lives inside `hbars()`, `donut()`, `legend()` and `table()` rather than at the ~40 call sites, so all of them obey at once and a dataset loaded from a file at runtime cannot break it. A table sorts on the currency column with the largest absolute total — three simpler positional rules were tried and each broke a real table. Three tables opt out with `order:'keep'`: two that list unlike measures down one column, and one whose rows are a pipeline in sequence. Time series and waterfalls are never sorted. Verified across 105 tables, 63 ranked lists and 25 donut+legend pairs on every screen in every dataset.

**Every column carries its own sort and filter, and the control is the header itself** — a ghost button around the label, always visible, on every table down to two rows. Both sort directions are named and worded by content (`Sort A to Z` on names, `Sort ascending · oldest first` on dates); the filter is multi-select within a column and AND across columns. Columns are classified from their own cells: rank words sort in rank order, figures and dates get quartile bands, sentence columns get a contains box instead of a checklist. The popover follows the reference vocabulary — icon-led rows, real checkboxes, a search field, a keyboard hint.

**A table row is the height of a row.** Slack pools below the table rather than being shared out among the rows; equal box heights come from the five-row clip, never from stretching content.

**Every plot answers a hover**, through a shared readout portalled to `<body>` with full-height hit bands rather than the 2px mark itself.

**It moves.** Bars rise, lines draw, donut arcs sweep, boxes stagger. A cold start plays a splash assembling the logo's four ribbons, then a greeting; a screen switch shows a skeleton. Toasts stack at the top right with a draining timer bar. Nothing animates on a filter change. `prefers-reduced-motion` and `?nofx` both land on the finished state.

**The reconciliation strip is a ticket** — notched, hairline-perforated, the equation grouped so it reads as one statement, stats on the counterfoil. Pinned, it becomes a header instead: full-bleed, no radius, no lift, collapsed to one line while *reserving* the height it gives up, and pinned at a **measured** offset rather than a constant, so it cannot come to rest underneath the bar above it. Collapsed and pinned it drops its chevron, because there it is a control that cannot act.

**An unmeasured workspace shows em dashes and a way in, never zeroes.** Every card whose source is not connected dims its header, names the cause, and offers **See how to connect** — a right-hand pane carrying the actual procedure for that step. The strip and the KPI tiles fall back to dashes with it.

**No KPI tile repeats the reconciliation strip any more — 93 tiles became 47.** On the Executive Dashboard six of the eight Metrics tiles restated a lane of the strip 200px above them, and the same 75% repetition held on six four-tile screens where it was simply less visible. The cause was the rule that built each screen’s counterfoil FROM that screen’s own KPI tiles so the two could never drift; that guarantee reads as waste once you are looking at both at once. What survives answers what the strip cannot: the Executive Dashboard now carries Realised Savings, Cost Per Employee, Technology As % Of Revenue and Unexpected Spend — an outcome, a unit cost, a proportion and a risk, rather than four sizes of the same question. **Every board screen carries exactly four of them**, which is the fix for what removing the duplication left behind: five screens down to a single tile, and a fifth tile elsewhere sitting alone on a second row. Seventeen new tiles were authored from fields the datasets already held, and four were dropped or folded into the tile whose numerator or denominator they were.

**Every figure now reads the same way: the period value, with a YTD byline under it.** That was the other half of the complaint, and half of that was a real bug — `Total Technology Spend · YTD`, `YTD Budget`, `Actual · YTD` and `Variance · YTD` all rendered the period-scoped figure under a YTD label, so narrowing to a quarter left the word YTD sitting over three months. The strip itself is untouched: it is the one component on all twenty screens, and the complaint was never that it was wrong.

**Each tile carries its own trend, inline and grey.** 76×32px at a 2.6px stroke beside the figure, drawing only the months the figure was computed from and answering a hover through the same readout every other plot uses. Inline rather than a band under the figure because that costs no height, which is what let it land on 139 tiles at once. Drawn in `--c1`, the theme’s own first data slot — so it is the accent under the default and Blue palettes and ink under Mono, and it matches the trend line and the donut on the same screen rather than sitting outside them. Never a status colour, which is the fault the earlier grey rule was actually guarding against: one colour on all sixty plots cannot signal anything, where green-up and red-down would be sixty status signals. **Curved, with monotone cubic interpolation** — the one kind of smoothing that cannot overshoot a point, so every high and low in a drawing is a month in the data. **The full-size charts take the identical curve**: a curved sparkline over a sharp-cornered trend chart is two line treatments in one product, and on the finance screen it was the same series drawn two ways 400px apart. Straightness is not the honest option it looks like — a straight segment asserts a mid-month path exactly as confidently as a curve, and both are interpolations; what matters is that neither invents an extreme. The harness walks every control point on all 184 interpolated lines to prove it. **It draws the months themselves, not a running total**, on the series’ own range rather than from zero: a cumulative line over positive months can only ascend, so the first version drew the same near-straight diagonal on two thirds of the board — and “month-on-month” is a request for exactly the movement that accumulating hides. A series whose whole spread is under 12% of its mean draws flat rather than being stretched into false drama. A tile whose figure is a structural count — Vendors, Active Contracts, Applications — gets no line at all, because there is no honest twelve-month history of how many contracts existed.

**The KPI tiles and the insight band are one tabbed region with one shape on every screen, and `Metrics` is the pane you land on.** Headline and two tabs on one row, four tiles in the Metrics pane, the ink band behind `Key Insights`, and one footnote — `View KPIs ›` inside the band. A matching one under the tiles was built and removed: the band is a single surface so a footer inside it is part of that object, where four separate cards give a control below them nothing to belong to and it floats however it is styled. There is no flat variant any more: five screens briefly stacked their panes because they were down to one tile, and a layout that changes between screens costs the reader on every navigation where a thin pane costs them once. The Data Model screen gained a region for the first time — it had no tiles at all, so its band had been rendering raw in the flow with no headline and no tabs. Landing on the figures rather than the commentary reverses an earlier decision, and it is the honest concession to the SME’s position: he thinks reading the numbers is the reader’s job, and nothing in the argument for keeping the band ever required it to go first.

**The band is pointers now, not prose, and it kept its ink panel.** The SME’s position was that insights are the user’s job and that no other dashboard does this — true of BI tools, false of management reporting, where no board pack or month-end review hands over figures without commentary. So the function stayed and the form changed — twice, because the first cut was not enough. It is now **one pointer per authored column**, only the derived first column carrying two and only when it has two different findings to carry, with every probe sentence rewritten so the finding stands alone and its implication is a droppable second one. Type, padding and the corner glow stepped down again; but most of the weight came off when the band stopped being what opens. **The band’s first cell is still derived** — `What You Might Miss`, from scored probes for concentration, when the variance accrued, the latest month against the year’s own pace, the fastest-moving line, the size of the tail and how many feeds the board is standing on, plus four screen-specific ones. Each finding contributes exactly one sentence and at most two of them print, so the cell is one or two lines and never more.

**The dataset gained a `monthly` block, and a flow is not a stock.** Eleven series across all six datasets — realised savings, revenue, anomaly impact, security spend, SIEM ingestion, forecast accuracy, committed spend, licences held and active, contract value, AI savings. A flow sums to its total; a stock ends at it (invariant 19), and getting those backwards does not throw, it silently prints a figure eleven times too large. They were generated from the totals they must reconcile against rather than hand-authored, because 66 arrays that each have to balance is 66 chances to be quietly $3K light. `zero` is twelve nulls, never twelve zeroes.

**Several figures that never moved are gone.** Committed Spend was `ytdActual × 0.694`, so every scenario reported the same commitment ratio however differently it bought; forecast accuracy was a hardcoded 94.2% on two screens; eight YoY and QoQ deltas measured against a prior period the schema has never held; Cost Per Transaction was the literal string $0.024; Oldest Open Alert was the literal string 11 days, on a feed whose rows carry no date at all. The forecasting strip kept its hardcoded 94.2% for one round longer than the tiles did, so the two disagreed on every dataset but the baseline. The first two are authored per dataset now, with monthly series behind them. The rest were deleted rather than apportioned — a figure the board cannot defend is worse than one fewer tile.

**Nothing in the product says it is a mock-up.** Thirteen such strings were removed on 30 July, under two rules: never claim something happened that did not, and where the disclaimer was the whole message, say what a real product would say. Verified against the rendered DOM of every screen in three datasets.

**Finn has a face, and it moves for a reason.** The mark is a hand-drawn creature — two black square eyes, an orange bar that is both arms and the central hub, two legs under it — replacing the two cropped logo blades it used to be, drawn at 30px so its motion is visible rather than theoretical. Eight states, one live at a time as a class on `#finn`, so all three places the mark is drawn move in lockstep: **frozen** while the conversation is closed (that stillness is a rule, and it reverses the earlier breathing mark — what still animates at rest is the cycling line in the composer), one pulse when a workspace with open alerts is loaded, springing up from its centre when opened, grown and held while you type or dictate, **its four limbs telescoping into a hub that never moves** while it works — arms leading, legs one beat behind, thickness constant — a quick pulse while words land, and one overshoot as a full stop. Blinks every 3.8–7.2s, suppressed while frozen and while thinking. Every state is bound to a real lifecycle event rather than a timer, so the mark cannot say something Finn is not doing. Geometry and timings are copied from a verified prototype in `planning/design-language/finn/`, never re-derived, and a harness diffs the mark byte-for-byte against it.

**Progress is one sentence, and nothing says it twice.** The three bouncing dots are gone — they animated the same fact the mark was already animating, in the same 40px. The byline itself is the indicator now: `Finn` **is thinking** for the opening beat, `Finn` **is about to answer** once the reasoning is done, then just `Finn` when the answer takes over. One name, one sentence, with a single box dancing at the end of the line.

**Every answer has things you can do with it.** **Copy** gives real plain text — blocks in text form, the working numbered, feeds named, tables tab-separated so they paste into a spreadsheet as columns. **Show the working** expands the derivation for that message alone, without moving the global Brief/Full switch. **Good answer** / **Needs work** store a verdict that persists with the chat, survives reopening it, and can be taken back; a downvote asks *why* from three real reasons and answers what it heard rather than saying "thanks for your feedback". The bar is ink and grey with a hairline above — the accent budget in this layer is still one and still the send button — visible on the last answer and faded back on earlier ones until hovered.

**Voice input is a real meter.** Fifteen bars scaled every frame from an `AnalyserNode` on the actual microphone stream, taking the cycling placeholder's place so the input becomes a listening surface; a CSS keyframe would have looked the same and would have been the canned-microphone version this feature was already rejected for once. Nothing is typed into the box until you stop talking — `interimResults` is off, so one final transcript lands at the end of the utterance instead of a half-heard guess being written and rewritten.

**It answers questions.** **Finn** rests as one composer in the middle of the bottom edge on every board screen, and is absent from sign-in, which carries no chrome at all, cycling what you could ask it. Clicking it raises three suggestions out of its own top edge; asking opens a surface at **~82% of the viewport**, with a maximise control for full screen. It greets by saying something it already knows about the workspace rather than by listing questions, and the **24 questions** — the four persona lenses, six each — sit behind one link.

**Brief and Full are about the answer, not the window.** Brief answers; Full adds `How I worked this out`, the numbered derivation with the real arithmetic. The thinking log plays that same derivation, step by step, before the answer lands — so it cannot show reasoning the answer does not contain — then collapses to `Thought for 2.4s · 5 steps`, still clickable. Plots are **inline**, each on its own tinted panel with a titled header. The veil under the resting composer warms to the accent when you click into it, and grows to ground the suggestions that rise out of the composer's top edge. A question records the screen, the filter scope and the dataset it was asked from. Chat history pins each chat's **resolution** — the money and the verb it ended on — above the question that produced it, with **View the complete chat**.

Nothing in it calls a model. Every answer is a `derive(D)` function, so all six datasets re-narrate for free and an answer cannot contradict the board; every plot is an existing plot from `charts.js`, with the real vendor marks. A question the data cannot support gets an admitted miss and three real questions, never an invented answer — and the microphone is real browser dictation where it exists and **absent** where it does not, because a mic that fills the box with a scripted question would claim something that did not happen.

**People have faces.** Eight supplied portraits, 20MB down to 703KB. One given name each, exactly the filename — no invented surnames on real faces, and the two stock portraits are recorded as stock in `avatars/README.md`. Transparent cutouts on a pale accent wash, so a photo gets the same disc an initials orb has.

**No table is hardcoded.** `S.cloud`'s optimisation rows were the last five, and they are derived from `D.opps` now — the empty workspace is what made it indefensible, since a workspace with no data still reported "Savings potential $78K".

**No control in the mock-up is inert.** The last clickable-but-dead breadcrumb — the ITFM compute drill — is a lineage label now, the same fix the anomalies one got. `.path` is gone from the codebase entirely; `.trail` is the only breadcrumb component.

**The screens beyond the original seventeen:** Sign in (chrome-less, with a real marquee), Team & access (summary, one table, and a right-hand side pane that both adds and edits members), Add a record (a form, not a board), and Getting Started (three chapters, a greeting, a designed video placeholder and five how-to-move-around pointers).

## Open items

None of these block a review.

1. **`identified`/`realised` only narrow under the Category filter.** An opportunity records a category and nothing else, so the other four dimensions leave those two figures at the whole-company total.
2. **Delta-direction semantics (`gup`/`up`) are per-call-site rather than per-metric.** Some tiles show a rising cost as green and others as red. What "good" means per metric is a content call.
3. **Chart text can't fully obey the §1 type scale.** SVG `viewBox` scales its own text, so the same 9.5px axis label renders larger in a wide card than a narrow one. The general fix needs measured rather than fixed chart widths.
4. **`--info` collides with the blue accent preset.** Only affects the comparison preset; orange is the chosen accent.
5. **A chart card can carry slack a table card cannot.** Mainly the finance waterfall (~165px at 1440). A broken axis was built and reverted — a bar that doesn't start at zero has to be read rather than glanced at. Recorded in Brand Guide §6; the slack is the accepted cost.
6. **One dense table scrolls inside its own card below 1280px** — ITSM's third-width "Incidents and infrastructure cost". The per-column header control costs ~13px per column, which is what moved this one over the line; a header was shortened to clear 1280. Others follow below ~900px. That is what `.tbl-scroll` is for; the page itself never scrolls sideways at any width.
7. **`fyMonthStart()` derives the fiscal year from `asOf`**, so a workspace early in its fiscal year is offered its custom-range calendar one year early. Cosmetic, popover only.
8. **`ASK` / `ASK_WHY` live in `screens.js`, not the dataset**, so Finn's next-year budget answer does not re-narrate when the scenario changes. The other 23 do.
9. **There is no prior year anywhere in the schema.** No question can be answered year over year. Round 14 deleted the eight strings that pretended otherwise — `+41% YoY`, `+11.2% YoY`, `+22% QoQ`, `+4.2% QoQ`, `+4.1%`, `−9.1% QoQ`, `−3.1%` and `+2.1 pts` — so nothing in the product now claims a comparison it cannot make. Finn's growth question was reworded to state contribution points instead, and says so.
10. **The manufacturing questions are still unanswerable.** The CIO brief asked about plants, metric tons, batches, production lines and manufacturing orders; the dataset describes a software company. Nine of those twelve need `plants` / `production` / `prior` added to the schema — deliberately deferred rather than faked, and Finn refuses them rather than inventing an answer.
11. **Finn's free text is a matcher, not a language.** Token overlap against the 24 questions, which refuses when the result is ambiguous. Replacing it is the job of a real implementation; the answers stay.

## Standing regression

`scratchpad/test-metrics.js` holds the two rules rounds 14 and 15 exist for: **no KPI tile may restate a lane of the reconciliation strip on its own screen**, and **every board screen renders exactly four tiles inside a two-tab region**. Both are checked on the RENDERED output across 96 renders — currently **380 tiles, 139 sparklines, 147 YTD bylines, zero repeats, no flat panels**. It also caps the band at one pointer per authored column. Its single exemption is named rather than skipped: a workspace with no products renders an empty state, because four tiles over nothing would be four em dashes. `test-ledger.js` asserts the same rule inverted — **450/450 stats are not duplicated by a tile**, where it used to assert that all 225 *were*.

**Four harnesses were found dead, not broken by round 14.** `.ledger-eq` and `.ledger-perf` were removed when the strip became six equal lanes with no seam, and `test-ledger.js`, `test-ledger-collapsed.js`, `test-widths.js` and `test-seam.js` had been throwing on a null box ever since — not passing, not running. The first three are repaired; `test-seam.js` is **retired rather than deleted**, since its entire subject is the seam that rule removed, and it now prints why it does not run. The two width harnesses then spent a round reporting a real defect nobody had been able to see: below 1300px the strip's gutters were 15px a side where 8 would do, and `Response Cost Per Incident` sat flush against `Cost Per Change` with no gap at all. Round 15 fixed it — every lane now reserves two label lines below that width, so a long label wraps without putting the six figures on six different baselines.


`scratchpad/regress.js` — 21 screens × 6 datasets × 3 palettes, plus every screen at 1280/1240/1200. Currently **441 renders, zero page or console errors**, and the only two flags are the ITSM table above scrolling inside its card at 1240 and 1200. `probe-narrow.js` covers 1100/900/768/430; `probe-stick.js` checks the pinned collapse doesn't flicker.

`scratchpad/test-finn.js` is the assistant's own harness — **288 answers** (24 questions × 2 modes × 6 datasets), zero errors, no `NaN`/`undefined`/`Infinity` in any rendered answer, plus the flows that are not a question: history and its pinned resolution, reopening a chat on a changed dataset, origin context, the thread surviving navigation, Brief/Full widening, the free-text match and the honest miss, Escape ordering, streaming with motion on, and the 430px sheet. It also asserts that no answer anywhere contains "mock-up", "demo", "prototype", "placeholder", "sample data" or "fake". **The two harnesses should merge.**

`scratchpad/test-finn-motion.js` is the newest — Finn's mark, its eight states, its progress line, its answer actions and its dictation meter. Its first and strongest assertion is a **byte-for-byte diff of the app's `<g class="creature">` against the prototype's**, because the whole motion system rests on that geometry being untouched and an optimiser or a formatter would break the animation while leaving the resting silhouette looking right. Then the nine constraints, measured rather than read: the hub's rect pixel-identical through a full thinking cycle, every limb matrix with `d = 1` and `b = c = 0` (length-only telescoping, no thickening, no shear), nothing rotating in any of the seven moving states, the arms mirrored within 0.02 and the legs lagging by 0.22s, three mark instances sharing one `animation-name`, the observed round trip `docked → listening → thinking → speaking → settle → idle` with nothing skipped, the blink timed at ~120ms and silent during thinking, and reduce-motion plus `?nofx` both leaving the mark drawn and still.

Round 13 added three more sections to it: the **byline sequence** (`Finn is thinking → Finn is about to answer → Finn`, with the word "Finn" appearing at most once at every point, no `.finn-dots` anywhere, and the dancing box the last element on the line); **all four message actions end-to-end** — copy's plain text carrying no tags or entities, the per-message working not moving the global switch, a like being undone, a downvote asking why and recording it, and the verdict surviving both a re-render and a commit; and the **dictation meter against real audio** — the harness writes a WAV, hands it to Chrome as the fake capture device, and fails unless the bars take more than four distinct heights. Currently **passing, zero console errors**: 15 bars, 11 distinct heights, peak 1.0.

The harnesses need `puppeteer-core@23` — **pin the major**: 24+ is ESM-only and needs Node ≥ 20.19, while every harness here uses `require()`, so a bare install fails before a browser opens. It lives in `scratchpad/node_modules`; nothing in `finoptic/` loads a line of it, and the mock-up still has no dependencies.

One thing it caught outside its own scope: `styles.css` carried an **orphaned declaration block** with no selector — four declarations, almost certainly collateral from the round-7 sweep that removed the thirteen mock-up strings. It styled nothing and the browser silently discarded it, which is why 441 renders never saw it. Removed, so the stylesheet is brace-balanced and that is now a usable check.
